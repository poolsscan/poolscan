## Ritme potongan

TOTAL: 15.6 seconds across 10 clips. The film has three tempos — breathe, accelerate, hold — and the whole argument lives in where it stops accelerating.

BREATHE (clips 1–2, 2.7s). Two longer holds. Cut 1 into 2 as the light band leaves the right of frame, so the eye is already travelling when the top-down lands. Hard cut, no dissolve.

ACCELERATE (clips 3–5, 3.9s: 1.5 / 1.4 / 1.0). Each cut lands slightly earlier than the eye expects — this is the overwhelm, built by editing rather than by content. Cut 3 into 4 on the densest frame, before the field settles. Cut 4 into 5 on stillness and near-silence, so the droplet enters against nothing.

THE ELISION (cut 5 into 6). The single most important cut in the film. Cut on the frame BEFORE the droplet touches. The impact happens inside the cut, so clip 6 opens with the pulse already leaving the centre. The viewer's brain supplies the strike, which makes the detection read as consequence rather than as an effect. Never show the impact here — clip 8 and clip 10 own impacts.

HOLD (clip 6, 2.0s). The longest uninterrupted hold in the film and the only place the acceleration stops. Do not cut inside it, do not trim it to match the rhythm around it. The film has been getting faster for four seconds and then simply plants its feet. That contrast is what makes this the most striking image, as much as the image itself.

THE VERDICT PAIR (clips 7–8, 3.5s: 2.0 / 1.5). Cut 6 into 7 hard on the moment the front crosses frame centre — optionally a 4 to 6 frame dip to ink black-green #12211D on the way under, never a fade to pure black. Let 7 run its full two seconds; the shot only works if it lasts long enough to feel bottomless. Then cut hard on the slap of 8. The asymmetry of the pair — a long descent against a short dead stop — is the argument. Do not equalise them.

DECELERATE (clip 9, 1.5s). Cut 8 into 9 on the dust settling. The rising camera lets the film exhale.

REST (clip 10, 2.0s). Full hold, no cut inside it. Let the outermost ring reach the frame edge and hold roughly half a second of near-stillness at the end. The brand end card is overlaid here in post, after the rings have formed — never before.

STANDING RULES. Always cut on motion, never on a static frame; if the selected second has no continuous motion vector (drifting particles, an expanding ring edge, the camera still travelling), reject the take and pick another. No dissolves anywhere except the optional dip on 6→7. No fades to black between clips. No speed ramps. If the film runs long, take the frames off clips 3, 4 and 9 — never off 6, 7 or 10.

## Tips generate

- Generate every clip at 5 seconds and harvest only the best 1 to 2 seconds. Budget roughly 6 to 10 generations for clips 6 and 10, 4 to 6 for clips 7 and 8, and 2 to 3 for the rest.
- Clip 6 is the film. If it fails, the film fails. Reject any take where the travelling front reads as a glowing ring, a light pulse, a shockwave or a radar sweep — it must look like a physical wave on water. If the model keeps adding glow, drop the words 'pale mint highlight' from the crest description entirely and let it be a plain wave; the mirror-breaking reveal is what carries the beat, not the light.
- Fallback for clip 6 if the see-through reveal never lands: generate a version where the front simply crosses the field and every pool ripples in its own signature (wide slow rings versus tight broken rings that rebound instantly), and cut it against a tighter insert of two pools reacting side by side. The comparison survives without the transparency.
- Clips 4 and 9 must read as the same location for the rhyme to land. - Blue is the enemy. Water is the strongest blue prior in any video model and 'underwater' in clip 7 is the worst offender. Expect to bin takes on colour alone. Plan a single grade or LUT pass over all ten finished clips at the end, graded against clip 1 as reference, pulling residual cyan out of the shadows and pushing the pine-green family.
- Clip 8's cracked clay will try to render brown, tan or ochre desert playa. If two rerolls still come back warm, generate it as pale grey-green polished stone with hairline fractures instead, and correct in the grade. Also watch that the crack bed reads as millimetres deep, not as a canyon.
- Clip 7 will try to add fish, coral, divers, bubble streams and tropical water. Reject on sight rather than trying to salvage; underwater is the strongest cliché magnet in the set.
- Clip 3 fails when the circles morph, wobble or bleed into each other. The beat only works if they stay hard-edged discs under fast onset. If onset speed causes morphing, generate it slower and speed the footage up in the edit.
- Clip 10 must be near-perfect to function as a mark. Reject takes with multiple droplets, an asymmetric or broken crown, off-centre rings, elliptical rings from a tilted camera, or spray. Slight overhead-angle error is fixable with a corner-pin in post; extra droplets are not. Generate more takes of this than anything else.
- Inspect every export at full size, corner by corner, before cutting. Generator watermarks and hallucinated marks on flat empty ground planes are the one form of text the prompt cannot suppress — clips 2, 3, 6 and 9 are the highest risk because they are mostly empty plane. Crop in slightly if needed.
- Because each clip is generated independently, surface texture, particle density and grain will not match. Add a single light film-grain pass over the whole assembled cut, after the grade, or the sequence will read as a stock-footage compilation rather than one film.
- Keep the generated ambient audio only on clips 1, 6, 8 and 10; mute it everywhere else and let a single quiet bed run underneath. If any generated audio contains music, a synth riser, a sonar ping or a sting, mute that clip entirely — those cues are added in post, deliberately, or not at all.

- Reject any selected second that reads as a still. Every clip must contain a visible continuous motion vector — a travelling ring edge, drifting motes, the camera still moving. Prefer a slightly less pretty take with real motion over the most beautiful frozen one.
